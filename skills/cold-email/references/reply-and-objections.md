# REPLY & Objection Handling

When a prospect replies, you're no longer prospecting — you're closing. Different rules.

## The 5 rules of a reply

1. **Diagnose the real objection first.** What are they *actually* worried about? Answer
 that, not the surface words.
2. **Don't re-pitch the whole offer.** 2–4 tight lines. They already know the pitch.
3. **Reframe if the offer was misunderstood.** If they think you sell X but you sell Y,
 correct the frame explicitly and early.
4. **Lead with them, not you.** Their situation → your answer to it.
5. **Close with 2 concrete timeslots in their timezone.** Never "are you free this week?".

## Objection map (quick reference)

| Objection | Real fear | Reframe |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| "My community can build it for free" | Why pay for something I can get free? | A volunteer can build software. They can't *own the outcome* — planning, testing, deployment, support, tuning. That's what stalls builds. |
| "Too expensive / what's the price?" | Is this worth it / can I afford it? | Price depends on scope; on a call I'll give an exact roadmap + quote. Worst case you leave with the roadmap and tools — free. |
| "We already have someone/tool" | Switching cost / risk | You keep what works; we plug the specific leak. Ownership at handover, nothing switches off. |
| "Send me more info" | Soft brush-off | One specific hook + 2 timeslots. Info lands better live in 20 min. |

## Worked example — "my community will build it for free"

**Context:** Prospect (education/community owner) watched a Loom, replied that members
would build the platform for free, and asked what makes us different + pricing.

**Key correction (real note from the team):**
> "We don't do platforms as the main thing — it's one of the bonuses. And the CTA is
> wrong: always suggest 2 timeslots in their timezone."

**The offer reframe:** it's not "we build a platform." It's a **90-day growth engine**;
the platform is one deliverable alongside a scale audit, a custom AI agent, a content
engine, and revenue attribution — and they own all of it at Day 90.

**Winning reply (tight version):**

```
Hi Krest,

Totally fair question — and it's great your community's willing to build it.

But the platform isn't really what we do. It's one deliverable inside a 90-day growth
engine we run for education brands: a scale audit that shows where revenue leaks today,
a custom AI agent working inside your email/calendar/CRM, a content engine that turns
one recording into ~10 posts a week, and revenue attribution tying content to sales.
You own all of it at Day 90 — code, systems, IP. Nothing switches off.

A volunteer can build software. What they can't own is the outcome — planning, testing,
deployment, and support, where most builds quietly stall. That's on us, so your team
stays focused on your members.

My guarantee: on a call I'll give you a full roadmap for your case + accurate pricing.
Worst case, you leave with the roadmap and the tools to scale — no cost.

Would Thursday 2 PM or Friday 11 AM (your time) work to map it out?

Best,
Ramez
```

**Why it works:** demotes the platform to one bullet inside a bigger system, handles the
"free" objection with the ownership-of-outcome angle, ends on a risk-reversal guarantee,
and closes with 2 specific timeslots in the prospect's timezone.

## Reply length guide

- First reply to an objection: 60–120 words. Enough to reframe, not a wall.
- Follow-up nudge (no response): back to ≤75 words, one hook, 2 slots.
